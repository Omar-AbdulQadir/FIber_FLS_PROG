Manual programming of the fiber flash memory

How to erase, program and verify the on-board Winbond serial flash by hand — with a CH341A USB programmer and a Tag-Connect TC2030 pogo-pin cable — when the STM32 test jig is not in the loop.

Read before you plug anything in

The flash is a 3.3 V part. Many of the common black CH341A boards drive their SPI lines at 5 V even when the socket is jumpered for 25-series operation. Confirm your programmer is a 3.3 V unit, or fit a level shifter, before it touches the board. Reversing 3V3 and GND will destroy the flash and may damage whatever else sits on that rail.

The board must be unpowered. The programmer supplies 3.3 V through pin 5. Leaving the board's own supply connected back-feeds the programmer and can brown out both.

02

The board-side connector

The fiber board exposes the flash on a bare 6-pad Tag-Connect footprint — no header, and no keying other than the outline itself. Orient it by the alignment holes: one hole at the pointed end, two at the flat end. Hold the board so the single hole is at the top, and the pad numbering below applies.

Orientation is the whole game

Pads 1 and 5 carry ground and 3.3 V, and they sit at opposite corners of the footprint. Get the outline the wrong way up and the plug feeds 3.3 V into ground. The three locating holes are asymmetric — one at the pointed end, two at the flat end — so the plug only seats one way. Trust the holes, not the look of the pads.

04

The J4 solder bridge

The board carries a two-pad solder jumper, J4, drawn on the schematic as a SolderCut. It ships open: two gold half-pads inside a single silkscreen outline, separated by a narrow gap in the solder mask. It stays open for the whole programming session, and you close it once the image has verified.

Closing it is the last thing you do to the board, after the image has verified and the programmer is off. Set the iron to roughly 330 °C and tin the tip lightly, touch both pads at once, and let a single small bead flow across the gap — a clean dome bridging the two, not a flooded blob spilling past the silkscreen. Step 11 has the detail, including the continuity check that proves it took.

Order matters

Bridge J4 after the image verifies, never before — closing it changes what the programmer is driving. On a reworked or returned board it may already have been bridged, so glance at the gap before you start: if it is closed on a board you have not programmed yet, find out why first.

05

Procedure

  1. Power down and isolate the board

    Disconnect the board’s supply and any fiber or data cabling. The programmer is the only source of power for the rest of this procedure, and the board must not be fighting it.

  2. Install the programmer software

    Download the CH341A bundle, extract it, and install the USB driver before plugging the programmer in:

    CH341A programmer bundle (Google Drive)

    Run CH341A/CH347 Programmer V2.21. Everything you need sits on the toolbar and the Chip Select row. Watch the status bar bottom-right — it reads No Programmer Connected until the hardware enumerates.

  3. Set the programmer to 25-series SPI

    On the programmer board, move the socket jumper to the 25-series position, as opposed to 24-series I²C EEPROM. In the app, leave Chip Select alone for now — step 5 fills the Type, Manufacturer and Part # fields for you.

  4. Seat the cable on the footprint

    Hold the plug with its blank face upward — the marked face goes toward the board. Line the three locating pins up with the three holes; there is only one way they fit. Press straight down until the pogo pins visibly compress, and keep that pressure on for the whole session.

    • Do not rock the plug — bent pogo pins are the usual cause of intermittent reads.
    • If you have a retaining clip for the no-legs variant, fit it now and free up both hands.

  5. Detect the chip

    Plug the programmer into USB — the status bar should stop saying No Programmer Connected — then press Auto Detect (25xx). A healthy W25Q16 fills in Winbond / W25Q16 and reports a chip size of 2 MiB, from JEDEC ID EF 40 15: manufacturer EF Winbond, memory type 40, capacity 15.

    • Nothing detected, or an ID of 00 00 00 / FF FF FF: the chip is not powered, or CS / SK / SI / SO are not landing. Re-seat the plug (step 4) and try again.
    • A different part detected: the board carries something other than a W25Q16. Stop and confirm the image is right for it before erasing.

  6. Back up what is already there

    Press Read, let it pull the full 2 MiB, then Save it as <board-serial>_backup.bin. Do this even on a board you believe is blank — it costs a minute, and it is the only way back if the new image turns out to be wrong. Archive it against the board serial before the board leaves the bench.

  7. Erase the chip

    Press Erase and wait for it to finish. A full chip erase on a W25Q16 takes several seconds, and the window may look frozen while it polls the busy bit. Do not unplug, and do not disturb the cable.

  8. Program the image

    Press Open and load the release .bin. Confirm the app reports the file size you expect and that it sits at address 0x000000, then press Program and let it run to completion.

  9. Verify

    With the same file still loaded, press Verify. This must pass with zero mismatched bytes. A verify failure is not something to retry blindly — see the troubleshooting table below. Do not move on until it passes.

  10. Disconnect the programmer

    Unplug the programmer from USB first, then lift the cable straight off the footprint. Do not solder anywhere near a seated plug — the pogo pins will not survive it.

  11. Close the J4 solder bridge, then prove it

    This is the last operation on the board, and it happens only once step 9 has passed. Bridge the two J4 pads as described in section 04: iron at roughly 330 °C, touch both pads at once, let one small bead flow across the gap, and let it cool undisturbed.

    Then check it. Put the meter on continuity and buzz from one J4 pad to the other. It must read closed, under 1 Ω. A bridge that looks right but reads open is the most common way a correctly programmed board still comes back as a failure — so do not judge it by eye, and do not skip the check.

    • Reads open: the bead is sitting on one pad only. Add flux and reflow it.
    • Goes intermittent when you press on it: cold joint. Reflow with fresh solder.
    • Inspect under magnification — the bead should sit inside the silkscreen outline, with no stray solder on neighbouring copper.

    Once the bridge reads closed, the board is finished and can go back for normal bring-up.

06

When it does not work

SymptomMost likely causeWhat to do
ID reads 00 00 00 No power at the chip, or SO is not connected. Measure 3.3 V at pad 5 with the programmer plugged in. If it is there, re-seat the plug.
ID reads FF FF FF SO floating high — pad 3 not making contact. Check pogo pin 3 for damage; clean flux residue off the pad.
ID changes between reads Marginal contact, or the board is still partly powered. Confirm the board supply is off, then apply firmer, straight-down pressure.
Erase never completes Status-register block protection is set. Clear the protection bits in the software's status-register editor, then erase again.
Verify fails in scattered bytes Clock too fast for the in-circuit wiring, or noisy contact. Drop the SPI speed if the app exposes it, re-seat, then erase and re-program from step 7.
Verify fails from one address onward The image is larger than the chip, or it was loaded at the wrong offset. Check the file size against 2 MiB and confirm the load address is 0x000000.
Board will not boot, but the image verified J4 is still open, or the bridge is cracked. Buzz across the J4 gap as in step 11. Reflow the bead if it reads open or intermittent.
Programming behaves oddly from the first read J4 was already bridged before the session. Look at the J4 gap. If it is closed on a board you have not programmed yet, find out why before proceeding.
Other devices on the board get warm The programmer's 3.3 V is back-feeding the rest of the board. Unplug immediately. In-circuit programming is only safe if the flash rail can be isolated.
